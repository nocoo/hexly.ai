THE DECISIVE MOMENT
An elephant leans its head into view, curling the end of its trunk gently upward to investigate one small folded paper airplane. Catch the instant the trunk tip almost touches the airplane's nose. The animal's broad ear and cheek provide most of the mass, with the trunk close beside the face. A natural quadruped neck base and just a little shoulder continue below the frame.

An 85 mm intimate elephant head portrait at a gentle three-quarter angle. The gaze and trunk-tip interaction receive attention; a broad ear overlaps the natural shoulder behind. It is a head portrait, not a scene of the complete elephant. These lens and focal-plane ideas describe composition and visual attention, not photography, fur detail, depth blur, or a scene backdrop. Choose the exact camera distance, overlaps, silhouette, tilt, and crop yourself. Make a strong compact logo, with the animal carrying the occupied area and the small accessory close enough to remain part of one mark.

COLOR AND ONE INTEREST POINT
Coherent blue-gray, slate, and soft mineral-gray planes across the elephant, with deeper desaturated blue shadows. The inner ear is a restrained warm gray, the eyes dark and clear, and any small tusks a solid warm ivory. No multicolored skin patches, pink giant ears, or rainbow trunk.
A single small folded paper airplane near the raised trunk tip, built from a few connected teal, coral, golden-yellow, and violet folded panels. It remains subordinate to the elephant and is the only colorful accessory. No bird on the head, juggling fruit, bubbles, stars, or extra objects.

DRAWING
Build the main animal surfaces from connected, irregular, flat color polygons and triangles sharing crisp edges. Large planes explain forehead, cheeks, ears, muzzle, neck or wings; smaller facets articulate expression. Controlled coherent light/dark planes imply volume, without beveled crystals, plastic shine, painterly fur, black outlines, or smooth 3D gradients. The facets must be visible across the animal itself. Avoid dense uniformly tiny triangles and square pixel noise. Clear silhouette and readable eyes come first.

FRAMING AND ANATOMY
Treat this as a charming wildlife instant inside a square viewfinder. Keep species-specific animal anatomy underneath the crop. Leave generous breathing room around eyes, ears, horns, trunk tips, and the complete accessory for a future 23%-radius rounded square. Do not crowd the top or rounded corners. Keep the complete ear edges, eyes, trunk tip, and airplane comfortably inside the eventual rounded-square tile.

Deliver exactly one 2048 × 2048 PNG at native high quality, on uniform pure white #FFFFFF. Solid opaque artwork with crisp antialiased edges.
