# Firefly 06 — A light taking flight

## Owner direction

The owner supplied `/Users/nocoo/Downloads/firefly.jpeg` and asked to preserve its graphic action while recomposing it in the established logo family. The image shows a single firefly hovering in front of a rising flame: a head facing toward the viewer's right, wings fanning laterally, and a luminous abdomen trailing down and right. The new icon needs a larger subject, a clean nighttime background motif, and much less pixelation than this reference.

The file is spelled `firefly.jpeg` on disk, although the owner's message called it `firely.jpeg`. A byte-identical copy is archived as `references/firefly-flight-flame.jpeg`. ## Composition and interest

Preserve the flying gesture and the relationship between the insect, the flame behind it, and the glowing abdomen. Enlarge the combined wing/body/flame group, rather than exaggerating the head or insect anatomy. Broad overlapping wings, a compact body, and the nearby flame should form one substantial mark occupying approximately 82% of the width and 84% of the height, with calm margins. The head turns toward the upper right; the abdomen trails toward the lower right. The wing span balances the taller flame on the upper-left side.

The external interest point is one separate rising flame behind and above the insect's left shoulder. Its movement and warm reflection connect it to the firefly's flight. The tail light alone is not the interest point. Keep the animal legible in front of the flame, with modest anatomy, an unobtrusive eye, and tucked limb hints. Do not add a smile or baby proportions.

## Family drawing and materials

Use connected irregular triangles and broad polygons across the head, body, wings, and flame. The edges are clean and antialiased, not stepped square pixels. Broad coherent planes create shallow sculptural volume, with finer divisions only where a joint or wing fold needs them. The result should retain Frogie's clear drawing structure and the reference boards' matte material and dimensional presence.

Keep natural amber, copper, bronze, and honey-gold dominant. Wing membranes use warm champagne planes with restrained cool teal and blue-violet shadow facets. The flame uses ember orange, apricot, and pale gold in flowing connected lobes, not solid crystalline spikes. Avoid decorative rainbow recoloring, glass shards, gritty surfaces, thick outlines, and masses of sparks.

## Night presentation

Generate an integrated square scene on a deep blue-green night field. A shallow crescent impression and a few widely spaced recessed star marks establish nighttime in the background's own tones, with one broad quiet cloud contour near an outer margin. This sparse night-sky relief is distinct from Frogie's flowing ribbons and Pew's tapered stripes. Keep it visible but subordinate; the reference's brown grain and scattered square pixels are not retained.

Soft flame and tail light illuminate adjacent surfaces; broad ambient light reveals the matte planes. The background fills every canvas edge without a baked-in frame or rounded mask. There is no landscape, log pile, podium, or UI. The scene is generated as one image because the requested dark setting and local light are integral. `sourceMode: "scene"` overrides the usual isolated white source. A flattened image does not contain independently recoverable foreground and background layers.
