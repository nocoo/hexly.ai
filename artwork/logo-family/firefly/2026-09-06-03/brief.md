## Why the composition changes

The owner rejected study 02 because it was visually sparse and unsuitable as a logo. Its long diagonal body, spread wings, thin limbs, and distant large flame expanded the outer bounds without creating a substantial central mass. The visible facets and external interest point did not fix that composition. Preserve that request and its original bytes as a rejected study.

Firefly remains a blog and publishing platform for sharing ideas. This is a new complete composition of the same animal, not a rescale or finish of study 02.

## A compact mark

One short, round firefly curls gently toward one tiny independent flame beside its face. A large friendly head overlaps a broad, squat body; rounded wing cases and partially folded inner wings gather behind the body into one substantial silhouette. The glowing tail turns inward under the belly. The animal has an almost upright three-quarter presence, with a modest inward lean rather than a long diagonal flight axis.

The animal alone must be nearly as wide as it is tall. Head, shell, and abdomen supply the area; antenna tips, spread wings, and the flame must not stand in for filled anatomy. The combined mark occupies about 80% of the square in both dimensions, with roughly half of the canvas carrying colored anatomy. These are prompt targets, not measured claims about the eventual result. Keep a clear outer border and rounded-corner safety while avoiding large empty areas within the mark.

The small flame sits in a shallow pocket at the right of the face, separated by a narrow white gap. The gaze and two short bent forelegs turn toward it in a protective gesture. It is about one sixth to one fifth of the animal's height and no more than a small fraction of its area. The firefly and spark should read as one visual cluster. Do not wrap a thin insect around a large hollow center, spread it into a flight illustration, or let the flame compete with the head.

## Anatomy, facets, and color

Use a friendly firefly beetle with a large rounded head, broad overlapping wing cases, a short segmented abdomen, and a restrained golden light-organ at its tip. Two short antennae and six tucked insect legs support recognition; far-side legs may be occluded. The visible face has a strong eye and a small curious smile. Keep the full small animal inside the canvas.

Connected flat polygons cover the main head, shell, wings, and abdomen. Broad teal, jade, petrol, and mint planes describe volume; smaller facets serve the face and joints. Limited coral, violet, and blue accents sit at a wing seam and at the base of the warm flame. The independent flame is the external interest point; tail glow and eye highlights alone do not qualify. Favor clean matte color planes over smooth 3D shading or uniformly tiny triangles.
