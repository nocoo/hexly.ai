Use case: logo-brand.
Create one beautiful, welcoming FIREFLY CHARACTER LOGO for a personal blog. Concept: "A little light" — a gentle firefly sharing one little spark of an idea. Design a highly simplified graphic animal character from a few rounded shapes. It must feel charming, calm, and approachable at first glance. This is a complete character redesign.

INPUT REFERENCES, IN ORDER
1. Frogie defines the family's visibly connected FLAT color facets, strong simple masses, warm expression, and playful external accent. Borrow that drawing language, not the frog, its pose, colors, or musical notes.
2. Pew reinforces broad flat facets and a clear character with a small colorful interest point. Do not copy the zebra, stripes, or portrait crop.
3 and 4. The boards guide approachable icon presence, deliberate composition, and matte paper tactility only. Do not copy their smooth 3D rendering or backgrounds. Output only the character and its spark on white.

CHARACTER DESIGN — GENTLE GRAPHIC ABSTRACTION
A simple rounded jade head flows directly into a short, soft pear-shaped body. Use TWO SMALL RELAXED CRESCENT EYES and one little contented smile, drawn as uncomplicated dark graphic marks on the face. No eyeball spheres, giant eye discs, iris rings, eye sockets, glossy pupils, compound eyes, mandibles, or pointed muzzle. The face should express quiet delight, with none of the magnified details of an insect photograph.

Two short antennae have softly rounded tips. Two broad petal-shaped wings overlap closely behind the body like a small folded cape, giving the silhouette breadth. Use only a tiny rounded forelimb hint and discreet rounded feet; hide the other limbs inside the design. No displayed array of six legs, exposed joints, claws, hooks, sharp feet, hard armor, narrow waist, segmented exoskeleton, or realistic wing veins. The bottom of the plump body has a warm golden tail-light area, a clear firefly cue together with the antennae and wings. Keep an animal identity; no human fairy, clothing, ghost, lamp fixture, or humanoid hands.

COMPOSITION AND INTEREST POINT
Use an almost upright three-quarter pose with a small inward head tilt. Broad head, body, and gathered wings overlap into one compact, gently asymmetrical footprint, comfortably filling the central 75–80% of the square. Fullness comes from the arrangement of simple friendly shapes, not giant eyes or an enlarged anatomical beetle. Preserve a calm outer white margin. Do not spread the wings far apart or create a large empty center.

Place ONE small separate flame immediately beside the character's cheek on the viewer's right. A tiny rounded forelimb curves gently beneath it, as if offering a little light. Keep a narrow clean white gap between the flame and the animal so it is a distinct external interest point. The flame is smaller than the face, about one sixth of the whole character's height. Give it a simple bent flame tip, warm gold/coral planes, and two restrained blue/violet facets at its base. No torch, wick, candle, container, smoke, orbit line, scattered particles, or second character. The smile, tilt, and small gesture connect the firefly to this spark.

FLAT FACET FAMILY, BRIGHTER AND SOFTER
Build the head, body, wings, and tail light from connected irregular flat polygons and triangles with crisp shared edges, plainly visible across the main surfaces as in Frogie and Pew. Favor broad planes, with smaller pieces only for important features. Use a gently rounded outer silhouette while the interior retains clean flat facets. No smooth 3D plastic, shiny crystal, glossy bevels, black outlines between polygons, dense uniform triangulation, tiny pixel noise, or sharp shattered silhouette.

The dominant color family is bright jade and mint, with medium teal shadow planes. Keep the large color areas light and welcoming, reserving very dark teal for the small crescent eyes and smile. Use honey and amber for the tail-light tip. Add a restrained coral/blue/violet sequence at one wing seam, related to the flame's small base accents. Warmth and expression come before anatomical detail. Preserve the refined matte printed-paper feeling of the family without realistic insect texture.
