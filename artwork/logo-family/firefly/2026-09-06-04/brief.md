# Firefly 04 — A little light

## Design correction

The owner rejected study 03 because the enlarged insect felt frightening. Its eye discs, prominent shell, and articulated hooked legs kept an entomological reading despite the fuller silhouette. The owner clarified that fullness cannot mean simply making an insect bigger.

This study rebuilds Firefly as an approachable graphic character. Preserve a firefly's small wings, short antennae, and warm tail light; abstract the other anatomy. Firefly is still a blog and publishing platform, with the spark representing an idea shared with the reader.

## Character and layout

Use a soft upright pear-shaped body, a simple round jade face with relaxed crescent eyes, and two overlapping petal-shaped wings gathered behind it. Join the head and body without a narrow insect waist. Two short antennae have rounded tips. Limb indications are tiny rounded forms; keep the remaining anatomy hidden in the silhouette rather than displaying six hooked legs. The character should feel quietly joyful and welcoming.

The broad body and gathered wings form a compact visual cluster. Give the character a modest three-quarter turn toward one small flame just beside its cheek. A small rounded forelimb gestures underneath the flame. Its tip remains separate from the flame by a clean white gap. The flame is the one external interest point, supported by the smile and gesture. Keep it smaller than the face and integrated into the overall footprint.

Use a calm outer margin and an almost square overall footprint. Fullness comes from overlap and coherent color groupings, not a macro crop, huge eyes, overscaled shell, or a dense tangle of appendages. No enlarged eye sockets, compound eyes, mandibles, hard armor plates, visibly articulated limbs, long pointed feet, or realistic insect texture. Do not replace the firefly with a human fairy, a ghost, or a lamp object.

## Drawing and palette

Retain plainly visible connected flat facets across the head, body, wings, and warm tail. Favor broad, irregular color planes and rounded outer contours. The internal faceting must not turn the silhouette into a spiky crystal or a uniformly triangulated toy.

Use brighter jade and mint as the dominant proposed animal family, medium teal for shadow planes, and restrained honey/amber at the tail. Avoid large near-black eye discs or extensive dark shell surfaces. Keep the face graphic: two relaxed dark crescent marks and one small smile. A few coral, blue, and violet fragments at one wing seam and the small flame's base provide controlled multicolored accents. The actual generated colors will be sampled after selection; the existing Firefly website tokens remain separate evidence.
