## Concept and composition

One complete small firefly banks around one separate little flame. The insect carries the lower-left visual weight; its head turns toward the flame in the upper-right negative space. The curved body axis, angled wings, and directed gaze imply a flight arc without an arrow, ring, or drawn motion trail. The flame is the external interest point: a small idea attracting a curious animal, separate from the firefly's own golden tail.

Keep the insect dominant. Its deep teal and jade planes occupy most of the mark, with pale mint wings, a restrained golden light-organ tip, and small connected coral/violet/blue facets. A friendly eye and rounded beetle silhouette carry the expression. Six short insect legs and two antennae remain compact. This is a complete small animal, not a large-animal head portrait.

The flame is roughly one fifth of the insect's height. Give it a clean, recognizable warm silhouette, a gold core, coral outer facets, and a restrained cooler base accent. It leans back toward the insect and stays visibly separated by white space. No torch handle, book, candle, fuel source, or second mascot.

## Family drawing language

Use connected irregular flat polygons throughout the head, shell, wings, and abdomen. Broad surfaces must have plainly visible hue/value breaks; smaller facets serve the face and joints. Preserve a readable overall silhouette rather than adding uniform tiny triangles. Matte print tactility and shallow relief may support the drawing; smooth plastic shading must not replace it. Fragmentation is structural, not a small decorative inset.
