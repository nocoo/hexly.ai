## Art direction

Capture the moment it turns toward the viewer while chewing the sprig and gives a quiet knowing wink. The animal's expression is primary; the flowers add motion and a secondary focus outside its main mass. The product remains a personal knowledge home for documents, people, and their connections; the flowers are identity, not a product description.

Use an 85 mm portrait lens as a composition analogy: an intimate head-and-shoulders view with comfortable working distance and natural perspective. Depth of field is a hierarchy analogy, with the face and flowers receiving attention and the shoulders remaining quieter. It does not request literal lens blur, fur, or smooth three-dimensional rendering. The drawing stays crisp and visibly fragmented across the animal.

The square is a viewfinder, not a shape to fill by stretching anatomy. Let the model determine the exact camera distance, turn, head tilt, overlap, and crop. A buffalo's short strong neck settles naturally into broad shoulders. Retain room around horns, ears, expression, and flowers for later rounded presentation.
