## Direction

Reinterpret the trainer in reference image 1 as a refined, connected-facet character portrait for Poké Pocket. Keep the red cap with its white brim and dark round ball emblem, dark tousled hair, red-and-ivory jacket, natural confident smile, and the friendly backward glance while holding a red-and-white Poké Ball close beside the face. This is a character portrait, not an animal and not a realistic human photograph. Capture the decisive instant just before an adventure: eyes clearly meet the viewer, the raised ball is the single secondary focal point. Use broad, crisp, connected polygonal color planes, nuanced sculptural volume and a restrained red, charcoal and warm ivory palette. Do not copy the thick black outlines of the reference. The ball supplies the focused bright accent; do not add rainbow face paint, confetti, extra balls or other accessories. Keep the head, cap, hair and complete raised hand large and legible, with generous room around their perimeter. An 85 mm portrait-viewfinder analogy guides the natural turn and relative scale; let the model choose the best camera and anatomy. Show just the shoulders needed to explain the gesture, entering naturally through the lower middle edge of the canvas, with the rest of the body outside the view. No closed-off circular bust or cropped cap/ball/fingers. Keep the surrounding field entirely uniform white, with no frame, shadows outside the character, background texture, text or watermark. Reference 2 defines the connected faceted drawing language, while references 3 and 4 define only the eventual tactile, offset presentation, which will be added separately. The supplied trainer image is the identity and pose reference, not a layout to trace rigidly.

## Separate presentation

Offset interrupted ball orbits and short curved seams, impressed into a warm vermilion paper field. The exact foreground, independent backdrop, shadows, ten export sizes, static comparison and `/logos/pokepocket` page remain separate deliverables.
